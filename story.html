THE ALFORDS · 1768–2008

The Alford family

Christine Alford Knight grew up in a farming family near Mount Hermon, Louisiana. She became a nurse, moved to Baton Rouge and spent thirty-three years in public health.

Before her, three generations of the family had lived near the Bogue Chitto River.

We begin with a possible earlier branch in North Carolina. That connection is still uncertain. The family line becomes clearer in Louisiana with Christine’s great-grandparents, John and Margaret.

1768 · North Carolina

Who was Julius Alford?

Julius Alford lived beside the Tar River in colonial North Carolina. He owned a plantation, was married to Lucy, and named seven children in his will.

We start with Julius because his son Jacob may have been the father of Christine’s great-grandfather, John, in Louisiana. That connection is still an open question.

1768 · North Carolina

Julius provides for his family

In 1768, Julius set out how his family should be provided for after his death. His will allowed Lucy to use the remaining property during her lifetime or widowhood, and directed income from the estate toward maintaining and schooling the children.

Jacob was to receive the home plantation on both sides of the Tar River, along with livestock and a breeding mare.

Seaborn goes to war

In May 1862, John and Margaret’s son Seaborn enlisted in the Confederate army, just before his eighteenth birthday. He joined at Camp Moore and served in a unit that became the 3rd Louisiana Cavalry.

By the following summer, he was at Port Hudson, where Union forces were besieging the Confederate position.

Meet the family

Margaret Brumfield Alford

1819–1885 · Christine’s great-grandmother

Margaret was John Alford’s wife and Christine’s great-grandmother. Her church obituary says they married in 1833, when she was about fourteen. At her death in 1885, it counted a husband, fourteen surviving children and forty-three grandchildren.

She joined the Methodists in 1853, was baptized into Mt. Pisgah Baptist Church in 1866 and later joined Bogue Chitto church. These changes in membership are among the few details we have of her life beyond her family.

Her maiden name, Brumfield, comes from later family compilations; the church obituary calls her Margaret Alford. Census and family records support John and Margaret as the parents of Seaborn, Christine’s grandfather.

Mary Lou Fussell Alford

1883 or 1884–1946 · Christine’s mother

Mary Lou Fussell Alford, also called Lou, was Christine’s mother. She married Esco in December 1899, and they made their home in the farming community near Mount Hermon. In 1930, their household included sixteen-year-old Tolbert and nine-year-old Christine.

Her father was Henry T. Fussell. Eliza or Liza Hobbs is her likely mother, although the death indexes disagree on the mother’s name. Her own birth year is also uncertain: records give 1883 and 1884.

She died at home on 4 February 1946, three days after Christine’s marriage date. Her obituary names her daughter as “Mrs. Christine Knight.”

Esco Seaborn Alford

1878–1958 · Christine’s father

Esco was Christine’s father, a farmer and dairyman near Mount Hermon. He was the son of Seaborn and Laura Alford. He also served his community as a church deacon and parish jury commissioner.

He married Mary Lou Fussell in 1899. By 1900 they had their own farm household, near other members of his family. Their daughter Christine later trained as a nurse in New Orleans.

After Mary Lou died in 1946, he married Lynnie Schilling, who died in 1947, and then Florence Bennett in 1951. He died after a stroke in April 1958 and was buried at Bogue Chitto Baptist Church.

Christine Alford Knight

About 1921–2008 · The descendant followed in this story

Christine grew up on a farm near Mount Hermon, the daughter of Esco and Mary Lou Alford. She graduated from Touro Infirmary’s nursing school in 1941 and was working in public health in Baton Rouge by 1942.

She married Charles Binford Knight in 1946 and continued nursing. Her work included coordinating clinics for children with disabilities across several parishes. She retired in 1975 after thirty-three years with the Louisiana Public Health Department, according to her obituary.

She died in 2008, having outlived Charles. Her later obituary gives her birth as 1 January 1921; an original birth record has not been found in this research. Accounts disagree on her exact death date.

Laura A. Ellzey Alford

1844–1903, reported · Christine’s grandmother

Laura was Seaborn Alford’s first wife, Esco’s mother and Christine’s grandmother. She and Seaborn married on Christmas Eve, 1866, and had ten children, according to his later obituary.

She was probably the daughter of John and Elizabeth Ellzey. Her surname appears in several spellings; Seaborn’s obituary calls her Laura A. Ellzy.

Laura died in 1903, after more than thirty-six years of marriage. Her gravestone is shown in the story, but its dates are difficult to read. The dates used here come from an earlier cemetery transcription, with her birth date also supported by a family Bible abstract.

Charles Binford Knight

Married 1946 · Christine’s husband

Charles Binford Knight was Christine’s husband. They married in Bienville Parish on 1 February 1946 and later lived in Baton Rouge.

In 1950, he was an assistant manager at a funeral home while Christine worked as a parish health nurse. Her obituary describes a forty-four-year marriage and says that he died before her.

Seaborn L. Alford

1844–1919 · Christine’s grandfather

Seaborn was a farmer near Warnerton and Christine’s grandfather. Census and family accounts identify him as John and Margaret’s son. He served in the Confederate cavalry, was captured at Port Hudson in 1863 and was released on parole.

He married Laura Ellzey in 1866. They had ten children, according to his obituary; Esco, Christine’s father, was one of them. After Laura’s death, Seaborn married Candacy Bankston and had three more children.

His homestead lay south of his father’s land. When he died in 1919, his obituary said his home was “in sight of the place of his birth.”

John Seaborn Alford

1807–1891 or 1897 · Christine’s great-grandfather

John was a Washington Parish farmer and Christine’s great-grandfather. He married Margaret in 1833, and they raised a large family. Their land lay near the Bogue Chitto River; one of John’s lots later contained the family cemetery.

He had a twin brother, Seaborn John, who became a Methodist preacher. John’s own son was also named Seaborn. Census and family accounts support the relationship from John and Margaret to this younger Seaborn, Christine’s grandfather.

The twins’ later family account names their father Jacob, but we have not securely connected him to Julius Alford’s son in North Carolina. John’s death year is also unsettled: later sources give 1891 and 1897.

Julius Alford’s will

Julius’s will names his wife Lucy and seven children: John, Isaac, Goodrich, Jacob, Job, Patty and Sarah. It provides for maintaining and schooling the children and for Lucy’s use of residual property during her lifetime or widowhood.

Jacob was to receive the home plantation on both sides of the Tar River, along with livestock and a breeding mare. The river word is faint in the manuscript; the displayed transcript retains the uncertain reading as “[Tar].”

The will was written on 14 July 1768 and proved in November 1771—accepted by the court after Julius’s death. It establishes this North Carolina family, but does not connect Jacob to the later Louisiana Alfords.

How this landscape was made

The map places historical land claims and grants beside the modern Bogue Chitto River. Their outlines were reconstructed from the 1851 township map and present-day section boundaries. Small boundaries are approximate, generally within about fifty metres; houses and travel routes have not been plotted.

Jacob’s claim and Seaborn’s homestead lie about three and a half miles apart, measured between their centres. The family’s land records span several township sections.

Colours distinguish John’s and Seaborn’s properties. The broken outline around Jacob’s claim marks a possible earlier family connection. The accumulating outlines compare land records from different years; they do not show uninterrupted ownership or inheritance of one farm.

Esco Alford’s marriages

The compiled marriage index records Esco Alford and Mary L. Fussell on 28 December 1899. It also records his marriages to Lynnie Schilling on 30 April 1946 and Florence Bennett on 23 May 1951. These dates have not yet been checked against the original marriage returns.

Mary Lou died in February 1946; Lynnie’s death is recorded in 1947. These later marriages help explain references to Mrs. E. S. Alford after Mary Lou’s death.

Mary Lou’s death-index entries name Henry T. Fussell as her father and Eliza or Liza Hobbs as her mother. Other family entries conflict over the mother’s name, so that identification remains qualified.

Seaborn J. Alford’s obituary

The 1884 obituary of Seaborn J. Alford describes him and John S. as twins, born on 11 October 1807 in Louisiana near present-day Osyka, Mississippi. It says their father Jacob came from South Carolina and died while they were children. Their mother is not named.

It also describes Seaborn J.’s Methodist life and work as a local preacher. The evidence supports identifying his twin John with Christine’s great-grandfather.

This account was written decades after the twins’ birth. It does not establish that their father was Julius Alford’s son or the Jacob of the Bogue Chitto claim.

New Orleans Christian Advocate · 12 June 1884

Survey for the heirs of Jacob Alford

The survey of 20 January 1831 records 636.54 acres for the heirs of Jacob Alford, beside the Bogue Chitto River. The claim originated with an earlier settlement claim. Jacob was dead by the time of this survey, but the heirs are not named.

He may have been the Jacob named as John and Seaborn’s father in the later family account. That identification, and a connection to Julius’s North Carolina son, remain unproved.

The outline on the modern map is a later reconstruction of the claim. The original survey drawing is shown separately, in its own proportions.

Jacob Alford’s deed

In this 1789 agreement with Simon Williams, Jacob sells his right in Julius’s estate, due to him after his mother’s death. It concerns an inheritance, rather than a sale of a particular tract of land. He calls himself the son of the deceased Julius Alford.

Together with Julius’s will, it establishes the father-and-son relationship in North Carolina.

It does not identify Jacob with the man later named as the Louisiana twins’ father.

Franklin County, North Carolina · 1789 · deed book 7, page 41

1850 agricultural return

The 1850 agricultural return lists forty improved acres, six hundred unimproved acres, four horses, 250 bushels of Indian corn and fifty bushels of oats. It values the farm at $300 and its implements at $100. “Improved” and “unimproved” are the census’s land categories.

The row is associated with this John Alford by comparison with the population census. It does not identify the farm’s location, prove ownership or show that it was Jacob’s earlier claim.
