John Seaborn Alford

1807–1891 or 1897

John was a Washington Parish farmer and Christine’s great-grandfather. He married Margaret in 1833, and they raised a large family. Their land lay near the Bogue Chitto River; one of John’s lots later contained the family cemetery.

He had a twin brother, Seaborn John, who became a Methodist preacher. John’s own son was also named Seaborn. This younger Seaborn was Christine’s grandfather.

Their father was probably Julius Alford’s son Jacob, from North Carolina. John’s death year is also unsettled: later sources give 1891 and 1897.

Margaret Brumfield Alford

1819–1885

Margaret was John Alford’s wife and Christine’s great-grandmother. Her church obituary says they married in 1833, when she was about fourteen. At her death in 1885, it counted a husband, fourteen surviving children and forty-three grandchildren.

Seaborn L. Alford

1844–1919

Seaborn was a farmer near Warnerton and Christine’s grandfather. He was John and Margaret’s son. He served in the Confederate cavalry, was captured at Port Hudson in 1863 and was released on parole.

He married Laura Ellzey in 1866. They had ten children, according to his obituary; Esco, Christine’s father, was one of them. After Laura’s death, Seaborn married Candacy Bankston and had three more children.

His homestead lay south of his father’s land. When he died in 1919, his obituary said his home was “in sight of the place of his birth.”

Esco Seaborn Alford

1878–1958

Esco was Christine’s father, a farmer and dairyman near Mount Hermon. He was the son of Seaborn and Laura Alford. He also served his community as a church deacon and parish jury commissioner.

He married Mary Lou Fussell in 1899. By 1900 they had their own farm household, near other members of his family. Their daughter Christine later trained as a nurse in New Orleans.

Esco and Mary Lou’s son Cleo Clabon was born on 8 July 1904 and died that December, at five months old. He was buried in the family cemetery.

After Mary Lou died in 1946, he married Lynnie Schilling, who died in 1947, and then Florence Bennett in 1951. He died after a stroke in April 1958 and was buried at Bogue Chitto Baptist Church.

Christine Alford Knight

About 1921–2008

Christine grew up on a farm near Mount Hermon, the daughter of Esco and Mary Lou Alford. She graduated from Touro Infirmary’s nursing school in 1941 and was working in public health in Baton Rouge by 1942.

She died in 2008, having outlived Charles. Her obituary gives her birth as 1 January 1921. Accounts disagree on her exact death date.

Jacob Alford, reported father of the twins

Died 1824 (reported)

Jacob was the father of John Seaborn and Seaborn John Alford, twins born in 1807. He was probably Julius Alford’s son Jacob, who left North Carolina and later settled near the Bogue Chitto.

He is believed to have died in 1824. A published family Bible transcription gives the date as 16 July. The twins would have been sixteen, although their later obituary remembers them as quite small when their father died.

The Bogue Chitto land was surveyed for Jacob’s heirs on 20 January 1831. If this claimant was the twins’ father, that places his death between the beginning of his settlement claim in December 1807 and the 1831 survey.

Jacob Alford, Pearl River land claimant

Settlement claim from December 1807; dead by 20 January 1831

James O. Cosby’s list of actual settlers, certified 7 June 1813, records Jacob Alford with one claim by settlement beginning in December 1807. The settlement month need not mark his first physical arrival.

A survey made for Jacob Alford’s heirs on 20 January 1831 measures 636.54 acres along the Bogue Chitto and cites certificate 41. The later resurvey connects it to Cosby claim 987. These are successive measurements of one claim; the survey proves the claimant had died by January 1831 but does not name any individual heir.

This claimant was probably Julius Alford’s son Jacob and the father of the Louisiana twins.

Short histories

Jacob’s settlement by the Bogue Chitto

A dated settlement became a federal claim and, later, a survey for heirs.

Cosby’s 1813 list records a Jacob Alford claim ‘by settlement’ beginning in December 1807. The date anchors the claim beside the Bogue Chitto River, in territory then governed by Spain. It need not be the day Jacob first entered the region. Congress’s 1819 law provided donation grants for qualifying claimants or their legal representatives on the commissioners’ lists; the record does not show an earlier Spanish permit for Jacob.

On 20 January 1831, a survey measured 636.54 acres for the ‘Heirs Jacob Alford.’ The drawing puts the river on the tract’s eastern side and the present Louisiana–Mississippi boundary at its north. An 1851 township plat identifies the same claim by Cosby number 987 and certificate 41. The heirs are not individually named, and successive survey measurements are not separate family grants.

Jacob was probably the father of John Seaborn and his twin, and Julius Alford’s son from North Carolina.

Records: How Jacob’s settlement became a land claim · Survey for the heirs of Jacob Alford · The 1851 township map · Spanish West Florida becomes part of Louisiana

The field and the two cemeteries

A field on an 1851 map, John’s later land, and two distinct burial grounds.

A government township map approved in 1851 marks a FIELD along the older Elisha Roberts claim. The drawn field extends into lot 1 of section 18. Seven years later John S. Alford received federal title to that 36.60-acre lot and two lots in section 17, totaling 93.84 acres. The map shows worked ground before John’s patent; it does not say who worked it. A farm return associated with John reports corn, oats and forty improved acres in 1850, but it does not locate that farm on this field.

The mapped Alford family cemetery falls inside the section 18 lot on a modern reconstruction of the patent. That placement does not tell us when the burial ground was established. Laura Ellzey Alford’s weathered marker belongs to this family ground. Bogue Chitto church cemetery is a separate place: Esco’s 1958 death notice explicitly gives his interment there, adjacent to the church.

Part of the field and the later family cemetery lay within the land John acquired; the exact site of his house is unknown.

Margaret’s life in three churches

Her 1885 obituary follows a Methodist profession and two Baptist congregations.

Margaret Alford’s obituary appeared in the 1885 minutes of the Magee’s Creek Baptist Association. It says she first professed religion with the Methodists in 1853. She was baptized into Mt. Pisgah Baptist Church in 1866 and, soon after Bogue Chitto church was constituted, moved her membership there. The text describes a sequence of congregations over more than thirty years, rather than a single church affiliation.

The same obituary reports that Margaret was born in Washington Parish on 9 February 1819, married John S. Alford in 1833, and died on 8 September 1885. It names a husband, fourteen children and forty-three grandchildren among those mourning her. Those numbers describe survivors at her death, not a count of all children born. The account was written decades after her birth and marriage, and it calls her Margaret Alford without giving a maiden name.

By her death in 1885, Margaret had belonged to Methodist and Baptist congregations over more than thirty years.

Seaborn’s service and parole

Two compiled military cards place him at Camp Moore and Port Hudson.

A Confederate muster card records S. L. Alford’s enlistment at Camp Moore on 13 May 1862, in Company K of the 9th Battalion Louisiana Partisan Rangers. The printed note says that battalion later became Wingfield’s 3rd Louisiana Cavalry. Seaborn L. Alford was John and Margaret’s son, born in 1844; he should not be confused with John’s twin brother Seaborn J., born in 1807.

Another card places S. L. Alford’s capture at Port Hudson on 9 July 1863. It summarizes a roll of prisoners paroled there on 12 and 13 July. The card does not say which of those two days was his exact parole date.

Period photographs show the fortifications and Union artillery at Port Hudson during the siege. None identifies Seaborn or his unit.

Three men named Seaborn

The twins’ reversed names and a younger namesake span two generations.

An 1884 Methodist obituary describes twin brothers born on 11 October 1807 near present-day Osyka: John Seaborn Alford and Seaborn John Alford. John S. is the man who later married Margaret and acquired Washington Parish land. His twin Seaborn J. became a Methodist local preacher and died in 1884. The obituary’s recollections of their birth and their father Jacob were written many decades later.

The third man, Seaborn L. Alford, was born in May 1844. Evidence from John and Margaret’s household, a family Bible copy and a later naming account supports identifying him as their son. His Confederate service cards place him at Camp Moore in 1862 and Port Hudson in 1863. His 1919 obituary names Laura A. Ellzy as his first wife; Esco appears as their young son in the 1880 household.

John Seaborn was Christine’s great-grandfather. His son Seaborn L. was her grandfather; his twin brother Seaborn John was her great-granduncle.

Esco brings Christine home

One newspaper column records both her nursing graduation and his trip to New Orleans.

In the Mount Hermon news column of The Bogalusa News for 29 August 1941, Christine Alford’s graduation from Touro Infirmary’s school of nursing is dated 20 August. The item says she was home for a three-week vacation and would return to nurse at the hospital. Its next item reports that Esco Alford drove to New Orleans on Wednesday to accompany her home. Together the two notices connect her new work in the city with a visit to her family community.

The first notice calls her father “Asco”; the following notice prints “Esco.” Both describe Christine’s visit home after graduation.

The photograph shows Touro around 1910, about thirty years before Christine graduated.

Christine’s public-health work

Directories, a census entry and a later obituary trace a long nursing career.

Christine trained at Touro Infirmary in New Orleans and graduated on 20 August 1941, according to a contemporary newspaper notice. It expected her to return there as a hospital nurse after a short visit home. By 1942, the Baton Rouge city directory listed her as a nurse for the Parish Health Unit; the 1943 directory did so again.

In the 1950 census, Christine Knight was recorded in Baton Rouge as a registered nurse in parish health, working a forty-hour week for government. Her husband Charles was an assistant manager at a funeral home.

Christine’s later obituary says she coordinated clinics for children with disabilities across several parishes, served thirty-three years with the Louisiana Public Health Department and retired in 1975.

Montgomery County, Georgia

Bogue Chitto River area

Land-claim landscape and later family community

Jacob Alford’s settlement claim begins in December 1807 beside the Bogue Chitto, then in Spanish West Florida. The 1831 survey for his heirs measures 636.54 acres; later John S. and Seaborn L. received separate land grants elsewhere in the township. The mapped outlines compare records from different dates. They do not show one inherited farm, continuous ownership or the positions of family houses.

Alford family cemetery

Family burial ground within John’s later section 18 lot

The mapped Alford Cemetery lies within the reconstructed lot 1 of section 18 that John S. Alford acquired in 1858. Laura’s weathered marker is in this family ground; a 1965 transcription supplies dates the photo cannot reliably show. The map placement does not date the cemetery’s establishment or identify John’s house. This ground is distinct from the cemetery beside Bogue Chitto church.

Bogue Chitto church cemetery

Church burial ground, separate from the Alford family cemetery

A 1946 Mt. Hermon notice reports Mrs. Esco Alford’s interment at Bogue Chitto Cemetery; identification as Mary Lou rests on her separate obituary and household evidence. Esco’s 1958 death notice says he was buried in the cemetery adjacent to the church. This burial ground is separate from the Alford family cemetery in Section 18.

Port Hudson, Louisiana

Seaborn L.’s recorded capture and parole site

Seaborn L. Alford’s compiled military card gives Port Hudson as his place of capture on 9 July 1863. It summarizes a prisoner roll of paroles on 12 and 13 July, without fixing his individual day. The displayed siege photographs show fortifications or Union forces; they do not show Seaborn or prove where within the site he served.

Touro Infirmary, New Orleans

Christine’s nursing school and reported first hospital work

The 29 August 1941 Mount Hermon newspaper column dates Christine’s graduation from Touro’s nursing school to 20 August and says she would return after a three-week visit home to nurse there. A photograph of the Prytania Street institution dates to about 1910. It shows the hospital before Christine’s time, not her class or the particular scene of graduation.

Baton Rouge, Louisiana

Christine’s documented public-health work and adult household

Baton Rouge city directories list Christine Alford as a Parish Health Unit nurse in 1942 and 1943. In the 1950 census, Christine Knight is a registered nurse in parish health, while Charles Knight is an assistant manager at a funeral home. These sources place work and residence in the city; the regional map does not trace her exact commute or sites of later clinics.

Records: Christine in the Baton Rouge directories · Christine and Charles in the 1950 census · Christine Alford Knight’s memorial

Mount Hermon area, Louisiana

Esco and Mary Lou’s family community; Christine’s reported birthplace

Christine’s later obituary says she was born on a farm near Mount Hermon around New Year’s Day 1921. In 1930 she appears with Esco, Lou and brother Tolbert in a farming household. The 1941 Mount Hermon column reports her nursing graduation and Esco’s trip to bring her home from New Orleans. Mary Lou died at home near Mount Hermon in 1946. These records do not locate a specific house.
