1768–2008 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

The Alford family

Christine Alford Knight grew up in a farming family near Mount Hermon, Louisiana. She became a nurse, moved to Baton Rouge and spent thirty-three years in public health.

Before her, three generations of the family had lived near the Bogue Chitto River.

We begin with a possible earlier branch in North Carolina. That connection is still uncertain. The family line becomes clearer in Louisiana with Christine’s great-grandparents, John and Margaret.

PART 1: Early records

1768 · North Carolina

Who was Julius Alford?

Julius Alford lived beside the Tar River in colonial North Carolina. He owned a plantation, was married to Lucy, and named seven children in his will.

We start with Julius because his son Jacob may have been the father of Christine’s great-grandfather, John, in Louisiana. That connection is still an open question.

1768 · North Carolina

Julius provides for his family

In 1768, Julius set out how his family should be provided for after his death. His will allowed Lucy to use the remaining property during her lifetime or widowhood, and directed income from the estate toward maintaining and schooling the children.

Jacob was to receive the home plantation on both sides of the Tar River, along with livestock and a breeding mare.

1789 · Franklin County, North Carolina

Julius’s son Jacob

In 1789, Jacob sold Simon Williams his right to a share of his father’s estate that he was to receive after his mother’s death. The agreement identifies him as “the son of Julius Alford deceased.”

Before 1807 · South Carolina

A family moves to Louisiana

John Alford’s father was named Jacob. According to a later obituary of John’s twin brother, Jacob came from South Carolina shortly before the boys were born in 1807.

The account says their father died while they were still children, leaving their mother a widow. It does not give her name.

1807 · Near modern Osyka

John and his twin brother

John Seaborn Alford and Seaborn John Alford were twins. Their later family account gives their birth as 11 October 1807, in Louisiana near present-day Osyka, Mississippi.

We follow John, Christine’s great-grandfather. He became a farmer in Washington Parish. His brother became a Methodist preacher.

1831 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

Jacob’s land beside the Bogue Chitto

A Jacob Alford had claimed land beside the Bogue Chitto River by 1807. By 1831 he was dead, and the land was surveyed for his heirs.

He may have been the twins’ father. His claim lay in the same township where John and, later, John’s son Seaborn acquired land.

PART 2: John, Margaret, Seaborn & Laura

1833 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

John and Margaret start a family

John married Margaret in 1833. The dates in her church obituary make him about twenty-six and her about fourteen. Their marriage lasted more than fifty years.

They raised a large family in Washington Parish. We follow their son Seaborn, who would become Christine’s grandfather.

Their son, Seaborn

Seaborn L. Alford was born in May 1844 and grew up in John and Margaret’s household.

He belongs to the next generation: John’s son, rather than John’s twin brother. He would farm nearby, serve in the Civil War and become the father of Esco, Christine’s father.

1850 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

John’s farm

John’s farm produced corn and oats. The 1850 farm return associated with him also lists four horses.

Most of its reported acreage was classed as unimproved: six hundred acres, compared with forty acres of improved land.

1851 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

Locating the family’s land

This 1851 map divides the township into older land claims and smaller numbered lots. John and Seaborn later acquired several of those lots.

Matching the lot numbers lets us place their land on the map. Their houses have not been located, but we can see how close the two generations’ properties were.

1851 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

A field on John’s later land

The surveyor marked a FIELD that extended into a lot John would acquire in 1858. The ground was already being worked seven years before his land grant.

We do not know who was farming it in 1851. John was living in the parish by then, but the map does not name the farmer.

1858 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

John acquires land

In 1858, John received federal title to several lots totaling nearly ninety-four acres. The green areas show these separate pieces of land.

The family cemetery was later recorded inside one of the lots. This was a family burial ground, separate from the cemetery at Bogue Chitto church.

1862 · Camp Moore · Port Hudson

Seaborn goes to war

In May 1862, John and Margaret’s son Seaborn enlisted in the Confederate army, just before his eighteenth birthday. He joined at Camp Moore and served in a unit that became the 3rd Louisiana Cavalry.

By the following summer, he was at Port Hudson, where Union forces were besieging the Confederate position.

1863 · Port Hudson, Louisiana

Captured at Port Hudson

Seaborn was captured at Port Hudson on 9 July 1863 and released on parole a few days later.

The photograph shows Union artillery during the siege—the forces opposing him. It gives a view of the war he experienced, although it does not show Seaborn or his unit.

1866 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

Seaborn marries Laura

After the war, Seaborn married Laura Ellzey on Christmas Eve, 1866. Both were about twenty-two.

They had ten children, according to his later obituary. One was Esco, the son through whom this story reaches Christine. Laura was probably the daughter of John and Elizabeth Ellzey.

1881 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

A farm near his father’s

In 1881, Seaborn received title to a homestead of about 135 acres. Its three lots lay south of the land his father, John, had acquired.

The two generations had separate properties within the same township. The map shows that proximity; it does not mark their houses or imply that one farm passed from father to son.

1885 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

Margaret’s life in the churches

Margaret had been married to John for more than fifty years when she died in September 1885. Her church remembered a large family: a husband, fourteen surviving children and forty-three grandchildren.

It also recorded her religious life. She joined the Methodists in 1853, was baptized at Mt. Pisgah Baptist Church in 1866, and later joined Bogue Chitto church.

Esco and Mary Lou

Esco married Mary Lou Fussell in December 1899. She was also known as Lou and was the daughter of Henry T. Fussell. They would become Christine’s parents.

By the following year, the newly married couple had their own farm household near Esco’s family.

Seaborn’s second family

Six years after Laura’s death, Seaborn married Candacy Bankston. He was sixty-five. They had three children: Jacob, Frankie and Agnes.

These were Esco’s younger half-siblings. He was already an adult with a household of his own when this second family began.

1919 · Bogue Chitto, Louisiana

A life spent near home

Seaborn died at home in November 1919, aged seventy-five. His obituary placed the house three miles south of Warnerton and said he died “in sight of the place of his birth.”

He had served in a war and raised children from two marriages, yet his home was still close to where he had been born. Christine, his granddaughter, would be born a little over a year after his death.

She was Seaborn and Laura’s granddaughter and John and Margaret’s great-granddaughter. This was the farming family she grew up in.

PART 4: Education, marriage & work

1941 · New Orleans, Louisiana

Christine becomes a nurse

By age twenty, Christine had trained as a nurse in New Orleans. She graduated from Touro Infirmary’s nursing school in August 1941.

Her education took her from the Mount Hermon farming community to a city hospital. This earlier photograph shows Touro around 1910, before her time there.

1941 · New Orleans · Mount Hermon

Her father brings her home

After Christine’s graduation, Esco drove to New Orleans to bring her home. The hometown paper reported a three-week vacation before she returned to Touro to work as a nurse.

Her working life was beginning in the city, while her family remained in the Mount Hermon community.

1942–1943 · Baton Rouge, Louisiana

Public-health work in Baton Rouge

By 1942, Christine was working for the Parish Health Unit in Baton Rouge. The city directory lists her as a nurse there in both 1942 and 1943.

After training in a hospital, she had moved into public-health work. Mount Hermon, New Orleans and Baton Rouge mark the places where she grew up, trained and worked.

More training in public health

Christine continued her education while building a nursing career. Her later obituary reports a public-health qualification from Peabody College in 1944.

She was already working in public health by then. This further training fits the career she would pursue for the next three decades.

1946 · Bienville Parish, Louisiana

Christine marries Charles Knight

Christine married Charles Binford Knight in Bienville Parish on 1 February 1946. She was about twenty-five.

The couple later made their home in Baton Rouge, where she continued nursing and he worked at a funeral home. She would be known as Christine Alford Knight.

1946 · Mount Hermon, Louisiana

Marriage and loss in the same week

Three days after Christine’s marriage date, her mother, Mary Lou, died at home near Mount Hermon. It was 4 February 1946.

The funeral was to be held at Bogue Chitto church. The death notice names her daughter by her new married name, “Mrs. Christine Knight.”

1950 · Baton Rouge, Louisiana

Two working lives in Baton Rouge

By 1950, Christine and Charles were living in Baton Rouge. She worked a forty-hour week as a parish health nurse; he was an assistant manager at a funeral home.

Christine had continued in her profession after marriage. Her public-health career was now well established.

Esco’s place in the community

Esco was a farmer and dairyman. He was also a church deacon and served as a parish jury commissioner.

He died after a stroke in April 1958 and was buried at Bogue Chitto Baptist Church. Christine, living in Baton Rouge, was among his surviving children.

PART 5: Christine’s later life

1975 · Louisiana

Thirty-three years in public health

Christine retired in 1975 after thirty-three years with the Louisiana Public Health Department, according to her later obituary.

Her work included coordinating clinics for children with disabilities across several parishes.

2008 · Christine’s family

Christine’s life, and the generations before her

Christine died in 2008. She had spent thirty-three years in public health, been married to Charles for forty-four years, and served with the Trinity Episcopal Church Altar Guild. Charles died before her.

Her father Esco, grandfather Seaborn and great-grandfather John had farmed in the Bogue Chitto area. Her own working life took her to New Orleans and Baton Rouge.
